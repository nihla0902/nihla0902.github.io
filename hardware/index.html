Electrical design package for a two-storey commercial building

A full distribution design: single-line diagram, schedules, earthing and lightning protection.

Prepared the full design and the report
Team
Individual project

Tools
QElectroTech (drafting)
Status
Complete: 93-page report with drawings, schedules and bill of quantities

The problem

Whoever installs or inspects a building's wiring works from its drawings and schedules, so they must be clear, complete and agree with each other. I wanted to practise producing a full package the way a design office would.

What I did

  • Drew the single-line diagram, the earthing and lightning protection drawings, the lighting and power layouts for both floors, and typical wiring diagrams.
  • Prepared the load schedule, the main distribution board schedule and the cable schedule.
  • Added design calculations, a bill of quantities, a material schedule and general specifications.

How the design is organised

I started from the connected load. An AC panel and a power board and lighting board on each floor add up to 45 kW on a 415 V, three-phase, four-wire supply.

The single-line diagram shows how the main distribution board feeds each of them. The schedules then list the breaker and cable for each feeder, and the layout drawings show where everything goes in the building.

Result

A 93-page report that follows the whole design from the load to the bill of quantities.

What I would improve

Next: add a worked calculation for each feeder (current, cable size, voltage drop and breaker check), so every cable and breaker in the schedules traces back to a calculation.

The problem

A residential building needs lighting, power, a lift and a fire pump, all fed safely from one supply. I wanted to practise a complete drawing set for a typical apartment building in Kerala.

What I did

  • Set the project basis: a 415/230 V, three-phase, four-wire supply, TN-S earthing, concealed PVC conduit with FRLS copper cables, and the standards to follow.
  • Drew architectural plans and matching electrical layouts for the ground to third floors.
  • Drew the single-line diagram and the schematics for the lighting, power, sub-main, lift and fire pump boards.

How the design is organised

Each floor has an architectural plan and a matching electrical layout, so fixtures, switches and points sit on the rooms they serve.

The distribution board sheets show the incoming breaker and the outgoing circuits of each board. For example, the lighting board is fed through a 63 A breaker and the power board through a 100 A breaker.

The sheets refer to IS 732, the National Building Code and the CEA regulations.

Result

A 16-sheet drawing set, from the project basis to the lift and fire pump panels.

What I would improve

Next: add a load and demand calculation and a cable schedule, so each breaker and cable size is backed by a calculation.

Automatic night lamp using a NAND gate

A logic-gate circuit, simulated and then built on a breadboard.

My role
Designed and simulated the circuit
Team
5 students built and tested the breadboard prototype
When
2025
Tools
7400 NAND gate IC, GL5537 photoresistor, LED, breadboard, PROTO circuit simulator
Status
Complete, working prototype

The problem

Street lights, gardens and public places need lamps that turn on at night without anyone switching them. We wanted to do it with the simplest parts possible: a logic gate and no microcontroller.

What I did

  • Designed the circuit and simulated it in the PROTO circuit simulator to check the logic before building.
  • The team then built and tested it on a breadboard.

How it works

A photoresistor and a fixed resistor form a voltage divider. The middle point feeds both inputs of a 7400 NAND gate, which makes the gate work as an inverter.

  • In the dark, the photoresistor's resistance is high, so the divider voltage falls to a low level. The inverter turns that into a high output and the LED lights.
  • In the light, the photoresistor's resistance drops, the input goes high, the output goes low and the LED turns off.

In my simulation I used a 1 MΩ photoresistor, a 1 kΩ fixed resistor and a 328 Ω resistor for the LED.

Result

The LED lit in the dark and turned off in the light, in the simulation and on the breadboard.

What I would improve

To drive a real lamp, the output would need a transistor or relay. A real street lamp would also need some margin around its switching point so it does not flicker at dusk.

Load flow analysis of a 3-bus power system in GNU Octave

Bus voltages and line losses of a three-bus power network.

Team

When
2025–26 academic year
Tools
GNU Octave
Status
Complete

The problem

Engineers need the voltage at every point of a network and the power lost in its lines, before the network is built or changed. The numbers come from nonlinear power equations, so a program has to solve them.

What I did

  • Entered the network data: three lines with resistance and reactance, one slack bus and two load buses.
  • Wrote the Y-bus matrix and the iterative solver in GNU Octave, a free MATLAB-compatible tool.
  • Calculated the line losses and plotted the voltage profile and the convergence curve.

How it works

Line data (per unit)
LineResistanceReactance
1–20.020.20
1–30.030.40
2–30.0250.25

Bus 1 is the slack bus at 1.05 per unit. Buses 2 and 3 are load buses drawing 0.8 + j0.3 and 0.5 + j0.2 per unit.

The solver starts from a flat voltage guess. At each step it calculates the real and reactive power at every bus, compares it with the scheduled load, and corrects the voltage angles and magnitudes by a small fixed step of 0.1, for up to 20 iterations.

I used this fixed-step correction instead of Newton-Raphson, which uses a Jacobian matrix to reach the answer in fewer iterations.

Result

20.95041–30.00793

Total0.02623

The maximum power mismatch fell from about 0.78 in the first iteration to close to zero by iteration 20, as the convergence plot shows.

Bus 3 ends with the lowest voltage, so it is the first place to look at for voltage support.

What I would improve

Next: replace the fixed step with a Jacobian-based Newton-Raphson solver, compare the two on iterations needed, and try a larger network.

AquaPulse: river water-quality monitoring with a map-based website

A ten-input ESP32 sensor system, simulated end to end.

My role
Built the full ESP32 simulation and part of the website
Team
5 students, final-year B.Tech project
When
2026
Tools
ESP32, Wokwi, Arduino C++, DS18B20 temperature sensor, TFT display, web dashboard
Status
Phase 1 complete: simulation and website prototype

The problem

Air-quality maps use colour so anyone can read them at a glance. River water quality is harder to find and harder to read. We wanted a monitoring network where a station's health shows up as one colour on a map, with the detail behind it for administrators.

What I did

  • Built the complete ESP32 simulation in Wokwi: sensor inputs, threshold logic, status grading, display and serial output.
  • Built part of the website with the team.

How it works

The station reads ten parameters: pH, turbidity, TDS, dissolved oxygen, chlorine, ammonia, nitrate, temperature (DS18B20), ORP and conductivity. In the simulation, potentiometers stand in for the analog probes, and ORP and conductivity are simulated values in the code.

Each reading is compared with its thresholds and graded normal, caution or danger. The station's overall status is its most severe grade. It appears on the TFT display and in the serial monitor.

On the website, each station is a coloured marker on a map. Administrators log in to a dashboard with station status, alerts and incidents, thresholds and registrations.

Result

The simulation shows all three system states, normal, caution and danger, on the display and in the serial output. The website prototype shows the map and the administrator dashboard in demo mode.

What I would improve

This is Phase 1. The sensors are simulated, and readings are not yet sent from a device to the website.

Next: connect real sensors, calibrate them, and send live readings to a database that feeds the map and the alerts.

Soil moisture monitor with a live web dashboard

An Arduino sensor circuit, simulated before it was built.

My role
Simulated the Arduino circuit and built part of the web dashboard
Team
3 students
When
2025
Tools
Arduino Uno, capacitive soil moisture sensor, Arduino IDE, Python (PySerial), Firebase Firestore, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Vercel
Status
Working prototype with a hosted dashboard

The problem

Farmers and researchers need to know how wet the soil is without walking to the field to check. We wanted the reading to be visible from any browser.

What I did

  • Simulated the Arduino circuit before it was built.
  • Built part of the web dashboard with the team.

How the data moves

Data moves through four steps:

  1. The Arduino turns the raw sensor reading into a moisture percentage.
  2. A Python script (PySerial) on a connected computer reads the value over USB.
  3. The script writes each reading to Firebase Firestore with a server timestamp.
  4. The dashboard, written in HTML, CSS and JavaScript and hosted on Vercel, reads the data live. It shows a moisture gauge, a graph of the last hour, and whether the device is connected.

Result

On 12 September 2025 the dashboard tracked the moisture falling from about 93% to 39% over one hour.

Open the live dashboard

The page shows the last recorded readings. It says “Device disconnected” when the sensor script is not running.

What I would improve

The Arduino has to stay connected to a computer by USB. Next: an ESP32 with Wi-Fi so the sensor sends its own data, more sensors, and an alert when the soil is too dry.
